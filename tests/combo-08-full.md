## Q7
<div class="stem"><p>According to Nyāya (Classical Indian school of logic), the fallacy involved in the argument “Sound is eternal because it is produced” is called:</p></div>

## Q7 - Options
(A) Savyabhicāra
(B) Viruddha
(C) Asiddha
(D) Bādhita

## Q7 - Hint
**Answer:** B  
**Topic:** Logical Reasoning – Nyāya Fallacies (Hetvābhāsa)

"Sound is eternal because it is produced": being **produced** actually proves the **opposite** (whatever is produced is non-eternal). A reason that proves the contrary of the thesis is **viruddha** (contradictory reason).

- Savyabhicāra – inconclusive (the reason occurs with and without the conclusion)
- Asiddha – the reason itself is unproven
- Bādhita – the conclusion is contradicted by another pramāṇa

## Q8
<div class="stem"><p>Given below are two statements:</p> <p>Statement (I): In the Nyaya syllogism, Pratijna corresponds to the “conclusion” of the Aristotelian syllogism.</p> <p>Statement (II): In the Nyaya syllogism, Upanaya corresponds functionally to the “minor premise” of the Aristotelian syllogism.</p> <p>In the light of the above statements, choose the most appropriate answer from the options given below:</p></div>

## Q8 - Options
(A) Both Statement (I) and Statement (II) are correct.
(B) Both Statement (I) and Statement (II) are incorrect.
(C) Statement (I) is correct but Statement (II) is incorrect.
(D) Statement (I) is incorrect but Statement (II) is correct

## Q8 - Hint
**Answer:** D  
**Topic:** Logical Reasoning – Nyāya Syllogism

Nyāya's five members vs Aristotle:

- Udāharaṇa ↔ **major premise** (universal rule plus example)
- Upanaya ↔ **minor premise** (application to this case) → Statement II ✔
- Nigamana ↔ **conclusion**; Pratijñā is the **thesis** stated at the start.

The key treats Statement I as incorrect, since the conclusion corresponds to nigamana.

> **⚠ Note:** Some textbooks say pratijñā "corresponds to the conclusion" (it is the same proposition stated first), which would make both statements correct, (A). The key's view: conclusion = nigamana.
